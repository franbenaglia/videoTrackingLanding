ObjectFlow Offline

On-device object detection, multi-object tracking and bidirectional line counting for Android. The app analyzes the live camera or a stored video, assigns persistent IDs to people and vehicles, and counts each one when it crosses a user-defined line. All inference runs on the phone; the app has no network permission.

Technical capabilities

What the project implements, as it ships in version 1.1.0.

  • Offline AI inference

    EfficientDet-Lite0, Lite1 and Lite2 models (Apache 2.0) bundled in the APK and run with LiteRT on the CPU. An optional GPU delegate falls back to the CPU automatically if it fails.

  • Real-time camera pipeline

    CameraX preview and image analysis share one viewport, so analyzed frames match what is on screen. Stale frames are dropped instead of queued, and dropped frames are measured.

  • Multi-object tracking

    IoU and center-distance association with short-term motion prediction keeps an ID through brief occlusions and when two objects cross each other.

  • Bidirectional line counting

    Side test by signed distance, a hysteresis band against objects oscillating on the line, an optional region of interest, and at most one count per object and direction.

  • Video file analysis

    Decoding with Android MediaCodec, rotation metadata handled, and every crossing stamped with its position in the video.

  • Local storage and export

    Sessions and every crossing are written to SQLite (Room) as they happen, so a crash does not lose the count. Results export to CSV through the Android share sheet.

How to test the application

  1. Download the APK Use the Download Android APK button on your Android phone.
  2. Open the file Android may ask you to allow your browser or file manager to install apps. This is Android's standard prompt for apps installed outside an app store.
  3. Install and launch Follow Android's installation screens, then open ObjectFlow Offline.
Minimum Android
Android 7.0 (API level 24) on an ARM device.
Internet
Not required. Everything works offline; the app does not declare the Internet permission.
Hardware
A camera for live mode. Video analysis works without one.
Account
None. There is no backend and no sign-in.

Suggested demonstration

  1. Open Live camera and tap Start. Grant the camera permission.
  2. Point the phone at a street, a corridor, or a video of people walking played on another screen.
  3. Drag the two ends of the counting line across the path the objects follow.
  4. Watch each person or vehicle get a box and an ID, and the A → B and B → A columns increase as they cross.
  5. Tap Stop, then View saved session to see the totals and the list of crossings, and share them as CSV.

To test without pointing the camera at people, choose Analyze video and pick any video on the phone that shows people or vehicles moving.

Technical architecture

A hybrid app: the interface is web technology, and everything that touches frames is native Kotlin. Frames never cross into JavaScript; only results and statistics do, about seven times per second.

  • Interface: React, TypeScript, Ionic, Capacitor.
  • Native layer: Kotlin, CameraX, MediaCodec, LiteRT, Room. The camera preview and the box overlay are native views placed behind a transparent WebView.
  • Domain logic: geometry, tracking, counting and CSV formatting live in a pure Kotlin module covered by JVM unit tests, with instrumented tests on a physical device for the detector, video decoding, storage and export.

Limitations. Detection covers six classes: people, bicycles, cars, motorcycles, buses and trucks. Counts are automatic estimates whose accuracy depends on lighting, distance, camera angle and occlusion. The app is distributed as a direct APK download, is not on Google Play and does not update itself.

This application is provided for technical evaluation. It has not been independently security-audited. It does not identify people: there is no facial recognition or licence plate reading, and no images are stored.
